Most lists of AI tools for students are lists of ways to avoid studying. This one is not.
Every entry leaves you with something to do: read the explanation, attempt the problem, write
the draft, open the source and check it says what you were told it says.

Grouped by the task rather than the technology, because the useful distinction is where an
answer came from. A tool that reads your uploaded PDF, a tool that searches indexed papers, and
a chatbot answering from memory are three different things, and mixing them is how bad
citations get into good essays.

Maintained by [TiorAI](https://tiorai.com/), which catalogues AI tools for a living.

## AI assistants

General assistants students actually reach for, and what each is better at.

- **[ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/)** — Explains a topic at whatever level you ask, and will keep going until it lands. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Claude](https://claude.com/product/overview)** — Handles long documents well, which suits reading sets and lecture handouts. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Copilot](https://copilot.microsoft.com/)** — Free access to a frontier model inside Windows and the Office apps. `Freemium` `Web` `Windows` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Gemini](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/)** — Tied into Google Docs and Drive, so it works where coursework already lives. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Merlin](https://www.getmerlin.in/)** — Browser assistant with a daily free allowance, useful when budgets are tight. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Monica](https://monica.im/)** — Several models behind one sidebar that follows you across any page. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/)** — Answers with numbered sources attached, so you can check before you cite. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Sider](https://sider.ai/)** — Reads the page or PDF you are on and answers questions about it. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`

## Reading and understanding

Tools that work from a document you supply rather than from memory.

- **[Anara](https://anara.com/)** — Reads alongside you and answers with a link back into the document. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Eightify](https://eightify.app/)** — Turns a long lecture video into timestamped key points you can jump between. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Explainpaper](https://www.explainpaper.com/)** — Highlight a passage in a paper and get it explained at your level. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Gemini Notebook](https://notebook.google/)** — Answers only from the sources you upload, and cites which one it used. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Humata](https://www.humata.ai/)** — Question a set of uploaded PDFs and get answers pointing at the page. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Mindgrasp](https://mindgrasp.ai/)** — Builds notes and questions from a video, a PDF, or a recorded lecture. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Recall](https://www.recall.it/)** — Saves what you read into a linked knowledge base instead of a bookmark pile. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[SciSpace](https://scispace.com/)** — Explains dense passages in a paper and pulls findings into a comparison table. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[SummarizAI](https://summarizai.ink)** — Chrome extension that adds summary, chapters, chat, and Study flashcards on YouTube lectures. `Freemium` `Browser Extension`
## Flashcards and revision

- **[Knowt](https://knowt.com/)** — Imports a set of notes and generates flashcards and practice questions from it. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Quizgecko](https://quizgecko.com/)** — Makes quizzes from pasted text, with question types you choose. `Freemium` `Web`

- **[RemNote](https://www.remnote.com/)** — Notes and flashcards in one document, so revision comes out of writing. `Freemium` `Web` `Windows` `macOS` `iOS` `Android`

## Maths and problem solving

- **[Desmos](https://www.desmos.com/)** — Graphing calculator that makes the shape of an equation obvious immediately. `Free` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[GeoGebra](https://www.geogebra.org/)** — Geometry, algebra, and calculus tools used across school and university courses. `Free` `Web` `Windows` `macOS` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Mathpix](https://mathpix.com/)** — Photographs handwritten maths and converts it into LaTeX you can edit. `Freemium` `Web` `Windows` `macOS` `iOS`
- **[Mathway](https://www.mathway.com/)** — Solves across most school topics, with the steps behind a paid tier. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Photomath](https://photomath.com/)** — Point a phone at a written problem and get the working, line by line. `Freemium` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Symbolab](https://www.symbolab.com/)** — Step-by-step solutions across algebra, calculus, and linear algebra. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`

## Finding and screening sources

Getting to the right papers, and deciding which are worth reading.

- **[Connected Papers](https://www.connectedpapers.com/)** — Draws a similarity graph around one paper so you see its neighbourhood. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Consensus](https://consensus.app/)** — Searches peer-reviewed work and reports where the evidence actually settles. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Elicit](https://elicit.com/)** — Screens papers and extracts findings into a table you can compare across. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Litmaps](https://www.litmaps.com/)** — Citation maps that keep updating as new work cites what you track. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Research Rabbit](https://www.researchrabbit.ai/)** — Follows citation trails outward from papers you already know are relevant. `Free` `Web`
- **[Scholarcy](https://www.scholarcy.com/)** — Breaks a paper into a summary card with its claims and figures pulled out. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Scite](https://scite.ai/)** — Shows whether later papers supported or contradicted the one you are citing. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/)** — Free scholarly search with extracted figures and a well documented API. `Free` `Web` `API`

## How tools are selected

A tool qualifies when it helps a student **do the work**: understand material, practise,
find and check sources, keep notes, write their own draft, or manage the term. It has to be
usable on a student budget, which in practice means a real free tier, a student plan, or an
open-source licence.

### The rule that does most of the work here

**A tool that produces the answer is not a study tool.** Homework answer services, essay
writers, and paper mills are excluded from this list, all of them, however widely used.

That is not a moral position taken for its own sake. It is what separates a list that helps you
pass a degree from one that helps you fail it expensively. Every tool here still requires you to
do something: read the explanation, attempt the problem, write the draft, check the source.

AI-content detectors are excluded too, for the opposite reason. They are sold to institutions as
certainty and they do not deliver it, and a list aimed at students should not recommend a
product whose false positives land on students.

### What the categories are telling you

The grouping answers a different question in each case, and the distinction is worth
knowing before you trust an answer:

- **AI assistants** answer from training, and will state a wrong thing confidently.
- **Reading and understanding** answers only from a document you supplied.
- **Finding and screening sources** searches an indexed corpus of real papers.
- **Maths and problem solving** computes, or shows working, rather than generating prose.

A number from a calculator and a number from a chatbot are not the same kind of fact.

### What is excluded

Homework answer services and question banks. Essay writers and paper mills. AI-content
detectors. Tools sold to institutions rather than to students, including plagiarism
platforms and proctoring. Anything with no free tier, no student plan, and no open-source
licence, because a list for students that students cannot afford is a list for someone else.

Developer tools are also out of scope. A student learning to program is better served by
`awesome-ai-tools-for-developers`, which covers that ground properly rather than in one thin
category.

Entries are ordered alphabetically inside each category. Position carries no meaning, and
there is no paid placement, sponsorship, or affiliate link anywhere in this repository.

## Disclaimer

Everything listed is built and operated by a third party, not by TiorAI. Pricing, plan
limits, and terms change without notice, and a tool that fits today may not next quarter.

**Your institution's rules come first.** Policies on AI use differ between universities,
between departments, and sometimes between two modules on the same degree. Nothing in this list
overrides them, and a tool being listed here is not evidence that using it is permitted on your
course. Check, and when a submission asks you to declare AI use, declare it.

Every tool here can be wrong. The assistants invent citations, the summarisers drop the caveat
that mattered, and the solvers make arithmetic slips. Open the source and check the working
before any of it goes into something that is marked.

Listing here is editorial selection, not endorsement.
